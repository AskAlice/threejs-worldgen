## Brief (ready for a human: training setup, compute and architecture are judgement calls)

**Category:** enhancement
**Summary:** Generate training data from the #33 classic erosion solver, train a small image-to-image grid-update model offline, port inference to WebGPU TSL compute, and measure whether it beats the classic solver on speed and quality.

**Background:**
- "A Neural Material Point Method for Particle-Based Emulation" (ICLR 2025 submission) maps particles to a fixed grid and predicts updates with an image-to-image network.
- Against GNS and DMCF it reports about 15 h training (down from 10 days), 10–100× less memory and 5–10× faster inference.
- Its experiments are 2D, small-domain and trained in PyTorch.
- The authors' code is an early research version with no stated licence, so write our own implementation and don't copy theirs.

**Questions to answer:**
- Can a model small enough for real-time WebGPU inference (a few ms per step at 256²–512²) match the classic solver's channels and fans over long rollouts without drifting?
- Does it stay deterministic and stable when rolled out for thousands of steps?
- Is the speedup worth the added training pipeline (dataset generation, PyTorch training, weight export, a TSL inference port) compared with simply tuning the classic solver?

**Deliverables:**
- A dataset generator driven by the #33 solver.
- A training script kept outside the published packages, e.g. a `research/` folder.
- Exported weights and a TSL compute inference prototype.
- A short write-up comparing speed, quality (against the classic solver and aerial reference photos) and stability, with a recommendation to adopt it, defer it, or drop it. If adopted, the same pipeline is the route to MPM snow in #18.

**Out of scope:**
- Replacing the classic solver unless the comparison clearly favours the model.
- 3D MPM.
